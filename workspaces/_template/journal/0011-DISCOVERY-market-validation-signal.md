## Finding

Informal conversations with NPOs, SMEs, and less-structured MNCs confirm that the product concept resonates across organization types. Respondents described it as "useful" and could foresee it being helpful if adopted.

## What was validated

- **Pain is real across organization types** — not limited to professional services firms. NPOs (volunteer/donor communication), SMEs (client coverage), and less-structured MNCs (departmental coordination) all identified with the coverage gap.
- **User-friendly setup is the #1 requirement** — respondents emphasized ease of adoption. This reinforces the email-first, no-app design.
- **Interest exists** — organizations can see the value proposition without extensive explanation.

## What was excluded

Companies that rely on **manual documentation and unofficial quotations** — those operating entirely on personal relationships, verbal agreements, and informal pricing — are not a good fit. The AI has nothing consistent to answer with, and the answers depend on relationship context that can't be captured in a knowledge base.

This defines a natural **documentation readiness threshold**: the product works best for organizations that have at least some repeatable, consistent answers to common queries, even if those answers aren't formally documented yet.

## What was NOT validated

- **Willingness to pay** — respondents said it's useful, but none were asked to commit to a price. "That sounds useful" is not the same as "here's my credit card."
- **Actual usage behavior** — interest in a concept doesn't predict adoption. People may find it useful in theory but not change their habits.
- **Pricing sensitivity** — the S$20-60/month range has not been tested against actual purchase decisions.

## Refined target market

Based on this signal, the target market is better described as:

**Organizations with communication volume AND documentation readiness** — they get enough inbound messages to feel the coverage gap, and they have at least semi-structured knowledge (FAQs, policies, price lists, standard answers) that the AI can work with.

This includes:

- Professional services firms (original beachhead)
- NPOs with volunteer/donor communication
- HR departments with policy documents
- Customer service teams with standard responses
- Operations teams with supplier/procurement FAQs

This excludes:

- Companies operating entirely on personal relationships and verbal agreements
- Companies with no repeatable answers (every query is unique)
- Companies where pricing/service terms vary by relationship

## For Discussion

- The documentation readiness threshold means the TAM is smaller than "all SMEs." Is the addressable market still large enough to sustain a business?
- The NPO segment is interesting but has lower willingness-to-pay. Should NPOs be a separate pricing tier or served through grants/subsidies?
- What's the minimum viable proof that converts "sounds useful" into "shut up and take my money"?
