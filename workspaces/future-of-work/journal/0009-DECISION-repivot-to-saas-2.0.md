# 0009 — DECISION: Repivot Sequor to the governed agentic-services layer ("SaaS 2.0")

- **Type:** DECISION
- **Date:** 2026-07-03
- **Phase:** analyze (repivot) → codify (workspace: future-of-work)
- **Author:** jack-hong

## Decision

Repivot Sequor **from** "workers become builders / re-interface the CLI for all work / replace the SaaS
suite" **to**: **the control layer that lets a non-technical _director_ safely put AI agents to work
across the systems a company already uses, and prove to an auditor what every agent did and who approved
it.** Integrate-and-govern (never replace); **governance is the product** (priced as compliance);
**director, not builder**.

## Why (grounded — see `01-analysis/12-saas-2.0-thesis.md`)

Triggered by the team's objection ("hard to make employees builders even at the harness level; SaaS won't
truly die") + owner directive to think through "SaaS 2.0." Four parallel web-research passes (2025–2026,
cited) established:

- Systems of record **survive** (Deloitte ≥5 yrs) and **harden**; incumbents **layer agents on top**, not
  replace (Microsoft/SAP/ServiceNow/Salesforce). The **app/seat** tier reprices; the **data** tier holds.
- The **builder fallacy** is real (no-code last-mile plateau survived AI; METR: AI made expert devs 19%
  _slower_). What democratized is **consumption** → the many become **directors**.
- Enterprise core = **integrate-and-govern over incumbents** (61% CIOs buy from existing vendors; Menlo
  n=495: 76% bought). Governance is the **#1 barrier**; **EU AI Act Art. 14** (Aug 2026) is the hard, dated
  tailwind.

Two pillars remain **speculative bets** to validate: the non-builder **director-as-buyer** persona, and
**standalone-vs-absorbed** (do incumbents ship good-enough native governance). Named as validation
priorities.

## What it supersedes / keeps

- **Supersedes** the `briefs/01-vision.md §3` "worker builds / replace the suite" framing.
- **Keeps + re-centers** the four moat pillars as the _director's_ product (M1 undo/trace, M2 posture, M3
  multi-human, M4 governed sharing); comms stays the wedge/lighthouse (Decision A), now the live
  **proto-director** loop.
- Keeps the ~80%-substrate-exists thesis + capability-first (Decision B).

## Resolves F4 (Sequor vs Praxis)

The evidence **favors Sequor's integrate-and-govern stance over Praxis's replace-the-suite stance** — the
sharpest divergence in [[0002-DISCOVERY-competitive-whitespace-conjunction]]'s sibling comparison
(`01-analysis/11-sequor-vs-praxis-comparison.md`). Integrate-and-govern rides the best-evidenced market
fact; replace-the-suite fights it. F4's live question narrows to: is Sequor the _neutral, cross-vendor,
director-usable_ governance layer that Praxis (regulated-enterprise, replace-oriented) is not?
