# Brief 02 — Repivot: Sequor as the Governed Agentic-Services Layer ("SaaS 2.0")

> Authoritative record of the strategic repivot directed by the owner (2026-07-03), grounded in
> `01-analysis/12-saas-2.0-thesis.md` (evidence-backed). This supersedes the "workers become builders /
> re-interface the CLI for all work" framing in `briefs/01-vision.md §3` where the two conflict. It is
> the input surface for the new pitch (`01-analysis/13-pitch.md`), the repivoted user flows, and the
> product.

## 1. Why we pivoted (the owner's steer)

The original vision bet that **workers become builders** and that the platform **replaces the SaaS
suite.** The team's objection — _"it's hard to get employees to become builders even at the harness
level, and SaaS won't truly die"_ — was correct, and the market evidence confirms it:

- Systems of record **survive** (Deloitte: ≥5 years) and **harden** with AI; incumbents **layer agents on
  top** of them, not replace them.
- **Building stays a thin specialist skill** even with AI (no-code last-mile plateau; METR RCT: AI made
  experienced devs 19% _slower_). What democratized is **consumption**, not building.
- Enterprises **integrate-and-govern over incumbents** (61% of CIOs buy AI from existing vendors; Menlo
  n=495: 76% _bought_, not built).

So the durable position is not "replace the suite / everyone builds." It is: **the governed
agentic-orchestration + oversight layer that sits ON TOP of the surviving systems of record, letting
non-builder _directors_ safely put agents to work — with every step traced, posture-gated, interveneable,
and audit-exportable.**

## 2. The new one-liner

> **Sequor is the control layer that lets a non-technical manager safely put AI agents to work across the
> systems their company already uses — and prove, to an auditor, exactly what every agent did and who
> approved it.**

## 3. The seven value principles (the spine of everything downstream)

Each is justified in the pitch; stated here as the canonical list:

1. **Integrate-and-govern, never replace.** We sit on top of the customer's existing systems of record; we
   make those systems our moat, not our enemy.
2. **Director, not builder.** The user approves, inspects, steers, and corrects agentic work — they do not
   write code, flows, or harness config. Building is done by a thin specialist class, vendors, and us.
3. **Governance is the product, priced as compliance.** Audit trail + approval-routing + accountability
   lineage is the core, not a feature — carried by the EU AI Act (Art. 14, Aug 2026), not by "convenience."
4. **Meaningful oversight at scale, not rubber-stamp.** Risk-tiered control (pause-and-approve for
   high-risk, supervise-by-exception for the rest) engineered so the director genuinely steers.
5. **Cross-vendor neutrality.** One approval + audit + control surface spanning _many_ systems of record —
   the thing incumbents structurally cannot offer (each owns only its own silo).
6. **Outcomes over seats — honestly.** Priced on deployable value/work, not per-login; but we do NOT bet
   the company on outcome-attribution (unproven at scale) — we price first for trust and deployability.
7. **Undo + trace = the trust surface.** Rewind any step, see everything, keep versions — because
   consuming work you did NOT build is impossible without it.

## 4. What this supersedes / keeps

- **Supersedes:** "non-coders configure/build objectives," "re-interface the CLI for all work," and any
  "replace the SaaS suite" framing.
- **Keeps and re-centers:** the four moat pillars — but re-cast as the **director's product**: M1 undo/trace,
  M2 posture governance, M3 multi-human+agent coordination, M4 governed sharing. Comms remains the wedge/
  lighthouse (Decision A) — now framed as the first live **proto-director** loop (a human approving AI drafts).
- **Keeps:** the ~80%-substrate-exists thesis (PACT/EATP/aegis/loom/csq) and capability-first (Decision B).

## 5. The two bets we must validate early (most speculative)

1. **The director persona** — does a non-builder line-of-business "director" actually exist as a _buyer/user_
   of an oversight surface, or does budget sit with technical governance teams? (Cheap test: instrument the
   comms wedge's human-approves-AI loop — that human is a proto-director.)
2. **Standalone-vs-absorbed** — is cross-vendor neutrality + non-technical usability enough of a moat once
   SAP/Salesforce/ServiceNow ship native governance on the data they own?
